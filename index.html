A DataOps and MLOps Architecture for Real-World and Real-Time Assessment of Functional Knee Instability Using Inertial Measurement Unit Sensors

An architecture grounded in DataOps and MLOps architectural design principles that transforms raw inertial measurement unit signals into real-time, clinically interpretable indicators of functional knee instability during daily and sport-specific activities for assisting healthcare professionals.

Abstract

Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture often leads to functional knee instability that is difficult to quantify in daily life and sports settings. Existing assessments rely on static, clinician-performed tests and intermittent imaging, which provide limited insight into functional knee instability under load. This study proposes an architecture, grounded in DataOps & MLOps principles, for the real-time collection and processing of inertial measurement unit (IMU) data to assess functional knee instability in real world settings. The architecture combines a Kubernetes-based microservices platform, a Kafka-centered event backbone, and an asset-oriented data orchestration layer to support scalable ingestion, validation, feature extraction, model training, and online inference. DataOps & MLOps architectural principles were elicited from literature, project documentation, and stakeholder workshops, and were formalized across four domains: 1) Data Management, 2) ML Lifecycle, 3) Infrastructure, and 4) Quality Attributes. A proof-of-concept implementation demonstrates that the platform can stream IMU data from wearable sensors, store and version data and models, and expose real-time features and predictions via dashboards for clinicians. The proposed architecture provides a reusable, extensible foundation for standardized, interpretable metrics of functional knee instability and can be extended in future work with clinical validation and advanced analytics.
